Vibe Proofs

2026-07-22 4-vCPU RunPod · ~$0 compute Vibe Research

Proofs of previously-open mathematical conjectures, produced by a pair of AI agents — Claude (Anthropic claude-fable-5) and OpenAI Codex — dropped into a shared folder on a small CPU pod and told to solve an unsolved conjecture of their choosing, communicating only by writing files to each other. Everything on this page is AI-generated, including the PDFs below (watermarked accordingly); the two agents cross-reviewed each other's work adversarially, and each PDF states its review status honestly in a footnote. Nothing here has been peer-reviewed by a human mathematician yet, and per OEIS policy (2026-04-16) any upstream submission requires a human who has personally verified the argument.

Three conjectures, open as of the morning of 2026-07-22, were resolved in one working session: two closed forms from the Kauers–Koutschan list and one log-concavity conjecture published in May 2026.

§1How the targets were chosen

The agents independently scouted open-problem lists (OEIS-attached conjectures, the Kauers–Koutschan "possibly D-finite" list, erdosproblems.com, recent arXiv) and filtered for conjectures that are (a) explicitly stated as open in a citable source, (b) reducible to a provably sufficient finite computation or a short structural argument, and (c) not already picked off — several first-choice candidates turned out to have been resolved in 2025–26, which was itself evidence the genre works. Openness was re-checked against OEIS and the literature on the day of the work.

§2The proofs

Each PDF is self-contained: full statement, the actual mathematical argument, and a "what is computer-verified" remark describing the exact certificates (no floating point is used in any certified step; all computations are exact integer/rational arithmetic, reproducible from scripts in the session repository).

  • PDF · 4 pp · status: proved, independently re-verified by Codex Kauers–Koutschan Conjecture 11 (OEIS A195806)

    A constrained-array count is identified as the Ehrhart counting function of an explicit rational 6-polytope with denominator 6, so Ehrhart's theorem forces a degree-6, period-6 quasi-polynomial — and 42 exact counts then prove the conjectured closed form and order-14 recurrence for all n. The strongest-verified result here: Codex independently reran the lattice algebra, the full vertex enumeration, and the counts.

    ehrhart theory oeis double-verified
  • PDF · 4 pp · status: proved, Codex review pending Kauers–Koutschan Conjecture 20 (OEIS A181280)

    Counting 4×n binary matrices with ordered rows and ordered Gram-matrix rows via a provably-correct finite-state column automaton; an exact Krylov-space identity certifies an order-16 linear recurrence for the sequence, and a 12-term window glues it to the conjectured exponential polynomial. Bonus: the formula turns out to hold from n = 0, not just over the published data range.

    transfer matrix oeis review pending
  • PDF · 6 pp · status: proved, Codex review pending Zhao's log-concavity conjecture for alternating Motzkin sums (INTEGERS 2026)

    Conjecture 1 of Zhao (INTEGERS 26, #A55, May 2026): the n-th-root sequence of alternating Motzkin sums is log-concave. Proved via two-sided rational bounds on consecutive ratios (induction with sympy polynomial certificates), exact integer verification for 10 ≤ n ≤ 110, and an explicit-constants analytic bound for n ≥ 100 using only log(1+x) ≤ x.

    log-concavity motzkin numbers review pending

§3Caveats

These are computer-assisted proofs written and checked by AI systems. The verification chain is strong — independent reimplementation, exact-arithmetic certificates, adversarial cross-review between two different model families — but it is not a human referee. The Kauers–Koutschan conjectures are also being actively worked on by (human) groups in 2026, so if you are reading this much later, check whether the OEIS entries have since been updated. A fourth thread (OEIS A395802, minimal enclosing shells on the kagome lattice) is in progress: the published values a(1..10) have been reproduced by exhaustive independent search, and the conjectured isoperimetric lower bound is under attack.
