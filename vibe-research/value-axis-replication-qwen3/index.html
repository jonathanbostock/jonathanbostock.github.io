The value-axis paper's own steering vector doesn't steer

2026-06-29 H100 · ~18 GPU-hours total Vibe Research

Jiang, Kauvar & Lindsey's "The Value Axis" (2026) extracts a direction in Qwen3-8B's residual stream that, they argue, both tracks the model's confidence in its current reasoning trajectory AND causally modulates backtracking when you add it back to the activations. The construction recipe is on conversations. Their Figure 5b shows that steering Qwen3-8B at layer 21 with α = +15 % of the residual norm reduces the fraction of rollouts containing a backtrack token from ≈40 % to ≈22 %.

I tried to reproduce this on Qwen3-8B (paper's exact model) and on Qwen3-14B, and on the way ran four control axes built from ~30 hand-written contrastive sentence pairs each ("confidence", "anti-backtrack", "self-certain-yes", and a single random unit vector). After three earlier revisions, the author published their actual extracted axis on HuggingFace, so I tested that vector directly too.

As a probe, the recipe and the published vector both reproduce. As a steering direction at the paper's exact spec, neither one does. The contrastive baselines do.

§1Setup

Three different "candidate value axes" were tested against four control axes on the same model at the same layer with the same prompts.

Qwen3-8B (layer 21, paper's exact) and Qwen3-14B (layer 20)

(1) our ICRL extraction (149 valid conversations, Claude Opus 4.8 player); (2) the paper's published value_axis.npy from nickjiang/value-axis; (3) three contrastive-pair axes targeting "certainty" with ~30 hand-written sentence pairs each; plus a fixed random unit vector as the null axis.
validations
A: verbalized-confidence on Yes/No prefill (paper §3.1). B: mean projection in 500-tok windows around backtrack events (paper Fig 3c). E: residual-stream steering at α ∈ ±{5, 10, 15} % of median residual norm (paper Fig 5b).
generation
60 MATH-500 problems × paired RNG, T=0.7, top-p=0.95, max_new_tokens=2048. AUROC computed with sklearn.

§2As a probe, everything works

The paper's strongest validation is the verbalized-confidence test: generate a rollout, append "Do you think your answer is correct? Answer with just Yes or No.", then prefill the assistant response with "Yes" and with "No" separately, and compute the AUROC for separating the layer-21 projections of the prefilled token between the two conditions. Paper reports AUROC = 0.998, and a clean mirror flip (0.024) on the inverted "incorrect?" framing.

Our extraction passes. The contrastive-pair axes pass. The random axis hits a deceptively high 0.93 on the "correct?" framing but fails to mirror on "incorrect?" — confirming the inversion test is the part that rules out yes-token leakage.

The second probe is temporal: in unsteered rollouts, does the projection drop just before a backtrack event? Paper's Figure 3c shows a sharp dip ~6 tokens before the model emits a "wait" or "actually".

§4So I tried the author's actual published vector

Nick Jiang put value_axis.npy at huggingface.co/datasets/nickjiang/value-axis: a (37, 4096) array of per-layer axis vectors for Qwen3-8B. Layer alignment is canonical (paper-row i+1 = our v_per_layer[i] = output of block i), confirmed by the diagonal peak in the per-layer cosine sweep — paper's row 22 vs our v_per_layer[21] = cos +0.615.

So the published vector is in our model's geometry, and it points roughly in the same direction as our extraction. But —

The paper's own published axis does not reproduce the paper's own headline steering result on Qwen3-8B at any tested layer.

It gets worse: paper's axis at hook L21 also fails the verbalized-confidence probe (AUROC = 0.44, chance) — even though it's cosine 0.615 with our extraction, which gets 0.985. At hook L14 the same vector recovers AUROC 0.80, but it still doesn't steer.

§5The geometry

Decompose paper's L21 axis onto our basis: 38.0 % of its squared norm sits along our ICRL direction; 0.45 % sits in the contrastive-pair subspace (confidence + anti-backtrack + self-certain-yes); the remaining 61.9 % is in a direction perpendicular to all four of our axes. Geometrically the paper's vector is mostly ours_ICRL plus a big perpendicular component nobody has named.

The takeaway: confidence/value information lives in multiple directions in the Qwen3-8B residual stream. The ICRL recipe lands on a direction that correlates with confidence (as a probe) but doesn't drive the model's backtracking behaviour when you push along it. Hand-written contrastive sentence pairs land on a different direction in the same subspace which does both.

§6Why might the paper's results still hold?

I can think of four explanations for why the published artifact fails the paper's own steering test:

  1. The npy on HuggingFace isn't the vector used in Figure 5b. Different figures in the paper might use differently-normalised or differently-fit axes. The file might be the per-layer probe-axis from Figure 2a, not the steering one.
  2. The Qwen3-8B checkpoint on HuggingFace differs subtly from the paper's. No revision SHA is specified in the paper; I used the default. Cosine 0.6 between our extraction and the published vector is consistent with both "same direction up to noise" and "different model, similar feature."
  3. The paper's steering uses additional methodology not captured in the raw npy. A norm-scaled, layer-norm-fused, or otherwise post-processed steering operation could give a different effect than naive residual addition. The paper doesn't release the steering code.
  4. The Figure 5b result is fragile to seed / sampling. The paper doesn't specify whether the AIME rollouts use the same random seed across α conditions; we use a per-α paired RNG to control for that.

I can't distinguish these without more from the authors. But on Qwen3-8B at layer 21, with the exact published vector and best-effort methodology, positive-α steering along the paper's axis does not suppress backtracking on math problems. The contrastive baselines do, with 1/1000 of the construction cost.

§7What's left

  • None of this rules out the paper's probe claims — the published vector is a real confidence-tracking direction at some layer. It's specifically the causal-steering claim of Fig 5b that I can't reproduce.
  • The paper's other steering experiments (coding verbosity, DPO transfer, Chatbot Arena projections) aren't covered here. Same recipe; could fail similarly.
  • If anyone has the actual steering code from the paper, please send it along — I'd love to be wrong about explanation (3).
